# Complete solutions for two-body collapse model of collisionless dark matter

Xu Z., 2026, *Complete solutions for two-body collapse model of collisionless dark matter*, Zenodo [doi:10.5281/zenodo.24093941](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24093941); the earlier version is hosted on arXiv as [arXiv:2110.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05784).

## Major findings

- The two-body problem reduces to one equation for an amplitude function F<sub>a</sub>(x) that measures the physical size of the pair, with three dimensionless parameters: the potential exponent n, a virial parameter γ and the ratio β of the Hubble velocity to the tangential velocity.
- Short-range forces (n ≤ −2) let a pair expand with the background (type I) or fall to the centre (type III); long-range forces (−2 < n < 0) produce bound pairs that oscillate about a fixed physical size (type II), i.e. stable clustering, for every such n.
- The critical overdensity Δ<sub>c</sub> = 18π² (32π² in the radiation era) is equivalent to an orbital period equal to the cosmic time.
- Mapping 956,476 two-particle haloes of an N-body simulation onto the model, 82 per cent are bound at z = 0; their mean radial velocity vanishes, as stable clustering requires, and their orbital phases are close to uniformly mixed where the orbits are resolved by the simulation.
- At z ≥ 2 the pairs are still falling in.
- A generalized stable clustering hypothesis for odd and even moments of the pairwise velocity holds in the simulation for the third moment but not for higher ones, which shows that infall and random pairwise velocities are correlated.
